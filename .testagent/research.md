## Timer/callback interleaving follow-up

Added seven cases (six methods) that interleave reads and writes rather than placing every assertion after all samples:

- Preserve the known zero-count hardware baseline when a timer reads before the first positive batch.
- Repeat after Reset and after an old zero baseline has aged out of the window.
- After inactivity, preserve a newly established zero baseline whether expiry was triggered by a timer read or by the new sample itself.
- Compare subsequent cadence with/without extra timer reads during a changing 24 s cadence sequence.
- Reading expired GPS speed must not destroy the distance anchor used by the next timely location.

Red/green evidence: the five zero-baseline cases failed against the previous implementation (expected 120 SPM, actual 0), while the two non-destructive-read control cases passed. After the parent fixed the expiry state, all 150 cases passed without changing their expectations. This exposed a genuine lifecycle gap despite the earlier 100% line coverage.

## Monotonic sensor clock follow-up

On-device debugging exposed a distinct integration issue: GPS UTC and the phone wall clock are not guaranteed to agree, so a good-accuracy location can be falsely classified as future-dated. Added 18 cases (13 methods) for the shared `SensorClock` and its composition with `DistanceTracker`:

- Explicit UTC anchor with nonzero device uptime (three days), current samples, delayed samples, and samples captured before clock construction.
- Sub-millisecond conversion at DateTime's 100 ns tick precision, including negative offsets relative to the anchor.
- Nanosecond `TimestampFrequency`, raw timestamps, and exact `GetElapsedTime` unit conversion.
- Repeated time reads leave the UTC anchor and existing sample mappings unchanged.
- Good current/delayed mapped samples are accepted; a sample older than the maximum age by one tick is rejected; a genuinely future monotonic sample by one tick is still rejected.
- GPS UTC skew of +250 ms, +90 s, and -90 s is demonstrated to fail with raw UTC but pass with the monotonic mapping. No permissive future-time tolerance was added to tests.
- Speed expires exactly at three seconds on the same monotonic clock and refreshes with the next valid observation.

New clock-specific tests passed 18/18, then the complete suite passed 168/168. The tests exercise the real Core composition; Android's wiring to `ElapsedRealtimeNanos` remains a device-integration responsibility. Values below 100 ns are outside DateTimeOffset's representation and are not claimed to be distinguishable.

Additional static mutation checks: changing nanoseconds-to-ticks division from 100 to 1000, dropping the boot-time anchor subtraction, reanchoring on reads, or returning an incorrect timestamp frequency would fail the explicit clock unit/offset tests. Replacing mapped timestamps with mismatched GPS UTC fails the clock-skew integration control cases.
